# REFLECTION — five-minute mission reflection (Day 29)

**Mission.** Help a busy staff engineer understand the evidence in under three
minutes.

**Fail condition.** A cold viewer cannot state what FAULTLINE proved. — *Not
triggered:* the final demo exposes a rubric-complete proof statement in a
108-second reading proxy, while the 591-word baseline fails and the 322-word
surface passes.

**The answer.** FAULTLINE proved one replay-verified corrective control: under
the same seeded simulator incident, configuration, and regression test, legacy
recovery stays red and exact validation plus quarantine and route diversity stays
green. It did not prove production provider independence.

**The strongest cut.** The short path omits the 29-claim article argument until
after the viewer can state the incident proof. The article remains one click
away; it no longer blocks orientation.

**The debug insight.** The incident did not fail at the first timeout. It failed
because stale fallback remained eligible after a known judge blind spot and
replanning reused the same dependency path. That distinction is why the trace
belongs in the demo.

**The honesty check.** The comprehension result is a deterministic structural
proxy, not a real staff-engineer study. Publishing that limitation prevents a
documentation test from masquerading as human evidence.

**Mastery gate.**

- **Can explain** — one sentence carries proof, mechanism, and boundary.
- **Can build** — live replay, timed cast, transcript, one-page case, and
  coordinated bundle.
- **Can debug** — the trace resolves false accept → repetition → re-entry →
  ceiling.
- **Can measure** — 165-second recording, 337-word case, five verified numbers,
  and baseline-to-final reduction.
- **Can defend** — Checkpoint 29 requires the cold-viewer answer and an explicit
  non-human-test caveat.

**What I would do next.** Give only the demo to one staff engineer, time their
answer to “what was proved?”, record any missing concept as a friction defect,
and update the rubric without changing the evidence claim.
