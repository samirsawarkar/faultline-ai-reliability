## Reviewer #2 — independent automated critic pass (separate context)

Inputs read: `paper/draft.md`, `evidence/claims_ledger.md`, `literature/literature_map.md`, `literature/research_gaps.md`, `experiments/results/p06_results.md`, and the four critic reports (`citation_review.md`, `methodology_review.md`, `statistics_review.md`, `reproducibility_review.md`) plus `claims_review.md`. Nothing else. No model APIs were run. Findings below build on the other critics' reports and do not restate them; where a point is adjacent to an existing finding, the existing ID is named.

**Central claim (my words):** On one synthetic 5-hop retrieval benchmark, repeated greedy executions of a high-accuracy model (R2) are statistically indistinguishable from independent Bernoulli trials, while a low-accuracy model (R4) shows a small excess of always-pass scenarios that does not survive infrastructure exclusion, so whether $\text{pass}^k = (\text{pass@1})^k$ is "regime-dependent".

**Three strongest reasons the central claim could be false, and whether the paper rules them out:**
1. The null the paper tests is not "independence"; it is "one common $p$ for all 150 scenarios and independent trials". Any across-scenario heterogeneity in $p_i$ produces $\text{pass}^k > (\text{pass@1})^k$ by Jensen's inequality, with no within-scenario dependence at all. With $k=3$ exchangeable trials the two are observationally equivalent. Not ruled out; not acknowledged (R2-001).
2. The R2 non-rejection has power only against large effects at $k=3$; the same ICC upper bound that the paper reads as "null" implies $\text{pass}^8$ more than double the naive prediction. Not ruled out; no power statement (R2-003; adjacent to STAT-009, METH-015).
3. "Regime" is one model, one time window, one gateway per point; the paper never tests that the two points differ. Covered by METH-005 and STAT-006; I do not repeat it, but it bears on R2-002.

FAILURE_ID: R2-001
CATEGORY: Claim integrity
SEVERITY: BLOCKER
CLAIM: "does the joint reliability over $k$ repeated executions equal the independence prediction $\text{pass}^k = (\text{pass@1})^k$, and if not, in which direction and by how much?" (§1, §2.1); "$C_k > 1.0$ ($\Delta_k > 0$) indicates positive failure concentration, $C_k = 1.0$ indicates perfect independence, and $C_k < 1.0$ indicates negative clustering" (§4.2); "Under independence, $c_i \sim \text{Binomial}(3, \text{pass@1})$" (§4.3)
LOCATION: paper/draft.md:§1, §2.1, §4.2, §4.3, Abstract, Title
PROBLEM: The construct under test is misidentified, and this is the paper's own construct, not a citation error. $\text{pass}^k$ as defined by τ-bench (and by the paper's own equation in §4.2) is $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i p_i^k$ under i.i.d. trials within task $i$. By Jensen, $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i p_i^k \ge (\frac{1}{N}\sum_i p_i)^k$ for every $k \ge 1$, with equality iff every $p_i$ is equal. Three consequences the draft never draws: (a) the "direction" half of the research question has a theoretical answer ($\ge$) that requires no experiment; (b) $C_k < 1$ cannot occur under the model the paper adopts except by sampling noise or negative within-scenario dependence, so §4.2's "negative clustering" reading of R2's $C_3 = 0.90$ is a misreading of noise; (c) the null hypothesis in §4.3, $c_i \sim \text{Binomial}(3, \hat p)$ with one $\hat p$, is the joint hypothesis "all 150 scenarios have identical difficulty AND trials are independent". A rejection (R4) is therefore evidence of scenario heterogeneity, i.e. that some scenarios are easier than others, which is what a benchmark is supposed to have. A non-rejection (R2) says the benchmark shows no detectable difficulty heterogeneity for that model at $n=150$, $k=3$, which is a statement about the benchmark's resolution, not about the agent. With $k=3$ exchangeable trials, heterogeneity across scenarios and dependence within scenarios are not separately identifiable (de Finetti), so the paper cannot attribute anything it observes to "failure clustering" in the agent as opposed to "some tasks are easier". The phrase "failure concentration" is the field's "overdispersion" renamed and presented as a finding about agents.
EVIDENCE: §4.2 equation for $\text{pass}^k$; §4.3 item 3 null specification; Table 2 (single expected-count vector per rung); CIT-012 already notes that the Jensen bound makes the draft's τ-bench sentence impossible, but neither the draft nor the other critics apply the same bound to the paper's own RQ, its $C_k<1$ definition, or its null. Ledger C025 ("consistent with independent trials") inherits the same misidentification.
WHY_IT_MATTERS: The title, RQ, Contribution 1 and both hypotheses are built on a comparison whose sign is known a priori and whose magnitude is a heterogeneity measure the paper does not name as such. Once restated correctly ("does this benchmark exhibit measurable across-scenario difficulty heterogeneity for these two models at $k=3$?") the question is routine and the answer is "not for R2, weakly for R4", which is not a paper.
REQUIRED_FIX: Restate the null as "homogeneous Binomial" throughout; delete the "negative clustering" reading of $C_k<1$; state the Jensen bound in §4.2 and reframe H4 as a heterogeneity/overdispersion test; remove "in which direction" from the RQ; retitle. If the authors want to speak about within-scenario dependence specifically, they need a design that separates it from heterogeneity (e.g. within-scenario perturbations that hold $p_i$ fixed), which this study does not have.
VERIFICATION_METHOD: Read §4.2–4.3 and confirm (i) the null is named as a single-$p$ homogeneous binomial, (ii) the Jensen inequality is stated, (iii) no sentence interprets $C_k<1$ as a clustering effect, (iv) the RQ no longer asks for a direction.
STATUS: RESOLVED
RATIONALE: Re-framed null hypothesis as homogeneous Binomial throughout; acknowledged Jensen's inequality in §4.2 and recognized that at k=3 exchangeable trials across-scenario heterogeneity and within-scenario serial dependence cannot be separately identified; removed speculative "negative clustering" reading of C_k < 1; added Tarone's score test for binomial overdispersion.

Also absent: any item-response / psychometric framing in which per-item difficulty heterogeneity is the default assumption rather than a finding.
EVIDENCE: research_gaps.md lists the search terms tried; none is "overdispersion", "extra-binomial variation", "beta-binomial", or "intraclass correlation binary". literature_map.md has no row for any of these. §4.3 and Appendix A.1 present the cell-collapsing dilemma as if no standard test existed.
WHY_IT_MATTERS: Novelty claim GAP-001/002 rests on a search that could not have found the prior work. A reviewer who knows Tarone's test will read §4.3 and Appendix A.1 as reinventing a 1979 result with a worse null.

**Novelty.** The observation that joint reliability over repeated trials exceeds the product of the mean when item difficulty varies is Jensen's inequality applied to a mixture of binomials, and the test for it is the extra-binomial-variation literature (R2-004: Tarone 1979, beta-binomial). τ-bench already defines $\text{pass}^k$ per task for this reason. **Recommendation.** Reject in current form for MLSys.
